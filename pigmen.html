Pigmentation Types Addressed

Different pigment irregularities stem from unique cellular pathways and require specific clinical protocols.

  • Melasma: Symmetrical, deep dermal patches influenced by hormonal triggers, requiring gentle, controlled management.
  • Sun Spots & Solar Lentigines: Defined surface spots resulting from cumulative UV radiation over time.
  • Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH): Dark marks remaining after localized tissue trauma, active acne flares, or scratches.
  • Patchy Facial Pigmentation: General uneven melanin distributions causing structural shading variance.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear medical explanations for managing uneven facial pigmentation.

What is the difference between sun spots and melasma?

Sun spots are caused directly by UV exposure over time and sit superficially on the skin. Melasma is deeper, more complex, and heavily driven by hormonal fluctuations and vascular factors alongside sun exposure, appearing as symmetrical patchy blocks.

Why is a doctor-guided plan necessary for pigmentation?

Using incorrect heavy lasers or unverified peeling agents can trigger rebound hyperpigmentation or worsen deep melasma. A doctor-guided assessment correctly identifies the depth of the pigment to build a safe, structured treatment pathway.

Will the pigmentation return after clinical treatments?

Melanin cells have memory. While clinical modalities clear existing visible pigmentation, maintenance routines combined with strict, daily application of broad-spectrum medical sunscreen are mandatory to prevent future pigmentation recurrences.
