Identifying True Keloids

  • Growth extending well past the initial boundary of the original skin injury
  • Firm, rubbery, or dense shiny nodules that resist standard moisturizing
  • Tenderness, severe hyper-sensitivity, or pain when friction occurs
  • Dark pink, dark brown, or highly vascularized red surface tones

Why Do Keloids Grow?

A keloid forms when the skin's biological cellular healing signal fails to shut down down post-injury. Instead of stopping at flat baseline repair, the body continuously pumps abnormal levels of thick collagen fibers into the wound site, creating an aggressive, raised mass.
